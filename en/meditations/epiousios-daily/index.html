Meditation 07

Epiousios — daily?

November 8, 2026

“Give us today our daily bread.”

Matthew 6:11 · World English Bible

We know this sentence, but what exactly are we asking? Behind “daily” is a Greek word whose meaning remains debated: epiousios. Some have read in it today’s bread, others tomorrow’s, and still others the bread necessary for our existence. Each of these interpretations places the emphasis on a different dimension: the moment when we need bread, or what is necessary for us to live.

Kenneth E. Bailey offers a light drawn from an ancient Syriac version. The word he notes there, ameno, expresses, according to him, what lasts, what does not cease. This reading makes it possible to bring the previous dimensions together rather than to choose between them. We could understand the request this way: “Give us today the bread we need, and which does not cease to come.”

We are not asking, then, for an infinite quantity of bread to receive now. We are asking for what is necessary for today, with the confidence that tomorrow, too, we will be able to receive what we need in order to live. The bread is sufficient, and the request bears on its continuity, not on its accumulation.

The nuance is important. We can have enough to eat today and remain preoccupied with tomorrow. In this reading, the prayer welcomes both realities: our present need and our worry before the days to come. It does not exempt us from planning ahead; it invites us not to seek all our security in our reserves alone.

To ask for this day’s bread is, then, to entrust to God more than a meal. It is to speak to him about what we live by, today and over time. Enough for this day, and the confidence to welcome the next.

Kenneth E. Bailey, Jesus Through Middle Eastern Eyes (IVP Academic, 2008), p. 121, as noted in James F. McGrath’s review.
